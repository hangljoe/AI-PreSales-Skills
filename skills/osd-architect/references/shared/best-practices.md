# OSD Best Practices

Distilled from chapter 8 of *The PreSales Handbook* ("The Discovery Summary / Opportunity Scoping
Document") and field practice. Read this before generating any OSD — it governs tone, scope
discipline, and how the document is used.

---

## What an OSD is for

The Opportunity Scoping Document is the **single source of truth** for an opportunity — the bridge
between the detailed, nuanced discovery and the solution, commercial, and delivery decisions that
follow. By leveraging it you build trust with clients, streamline internal processes, and increase
the likelihood of closing deals.

### The six benefits

| Benefit | Why it matters |
|---------|----------------|
| **Structure** | Structures complex information and guides discovery, ensuring completeness. Writing it down forces strategic thinking. |
| **Clarity** | Captures vital information from the first discovery call, avoiding guesswork later. Provides a clear, tangible reference so everyone shares the same information. |
| **Efficiency** | One structure familiar to everyone, a single source of truth. Faster support, more efficient reviews, reduced lead times. |
| **Control** | Take control with a structured approach — shape the opportunity toward your solution's strengths and avoid scope creep. Clearly define scope to keep everyone aligned and prevent costly deviations. |
| **Alignment** | *Internal:* all stakeholders work toward the same goals with a unified understanding of the client's needs. *External:* builds client trust through tangible documentation and supports highly targeted demos that resonate with the client's pain points. |
| **Protection** | Documenting your work creates clear records and prevents misunderstanding. |

## How to work with the OSD

### Start early
- Start the OSD **after opportunity assignment / first handover** and link it from the CRM opportunity for easy access.
- Use the OSD to **prep for discovery** — or fill it **during** discovery.
- Use it to **structure RFX information**.

### Reflection over regurgitation (handbook 8.3)
- Don't transcribe discovery — **interpret** it. Show that you understood the implications and root causes.
- Write from the **prospect's perspective**, so the prospect recognises their own business in it.

### Single source / update
- Update the OSD **regularly** — plan update time after calls, or set a weekly blocker.
- **Avoid other documents** — add information to the OSD ASAP; there is time to structure it later.
- For **multi-product deals**, the **lead SC is responsible for merging** the per-product input into one document.
- Keep the deal folder synced locally for fast access.

### Collaborate
- Use the OSD as the reference when collaborating with other teams.
- **Reference the OSD by link — never attach it** — in messages, calls, and deal reviews.
- Copy sections and share **externally** with prospects to validate flows, etc. (only the sections marked shareable). A prospect who reviews the OSD corrects misunderstandings early (handbook 8.3 validation, 8.4 feedback).
- Encourage **PS to update the OSD** after the implementation planning workshop.

## Considerations and don'ts (handbook 8.5, plus kit rules)

- The OSD should **NOT be a dumping ground** for everything a prospect sends (8.5: the trap of excessive detail).
- When including prospect collateral, **label it clearly as such**, with callouts overlaid to explain its relevance (kit).
- **Prospect vs your company's language** must be clearly cross-referenced — don't blur the two (kit).
- Don't let it go stale — an outdated OSD is worse than none, because people trust it (8.5: the oversight of stagnation).

## The canonical OSD sections (handbook chapter 8.2)

Every OSD is built from these sections (in order). Sections marked **\*** can be shared externally.

| # | Section | Purpose |
|---|---------|---------|
| — | **Document History** | Versions, modification dates, and contributors, so everyone knows whether content is current. |
| 1 | **Executive Summary** | Snapshot of the company, its principal challenges, and how your offering addresses them; fit, risks, gaps. |
| 2 | **Company Profile** | Size, industry, current solutions, key stakeholders, organisational structure — the client map. |
| 3 | **Goals, Challenges & Major Pain Points** | The issues, their implications, and root causes, with value mapping. |
| 4 | **Desired Outcomes & Vision \*** | Objectives, success criteria, value proposition, and the To-Be process flows. |
| 5 | **Supply Chain / Business Flow Map & As-Is \*** | How products, services, or information flow; current process flows. |
| 6 | **IT Overview & Architecture \*** | Current systems, integrations, legacy constraints, and the To-Be architecture. |
| 7 | **Project Prioritisation & Business Releases \*** | High-level rollout sequence, started by the SC and refined later by PS. |
| 8 | **Assumptions \* & Gaps** | Assumptions supporting the scope; gaps documented with backlog tickets. |
| 9 | **Modules & Detailed Scoping** | Product-specific module map and scoping questionnaire, aligned with PS to support estimation. |
| 10 | **Meeting Notes** | Decisions, insights, and actions with owners — the pulse of the OSD. |
| 11 | **Transition to Delivery** | Additional element: key players, commercial terms, and services strategy for the PS handover. |

Product- or team-specific sections can be added — the handbook treats the structure as a scaffold, not a mould.

## Templates and versioning

- Keep one OSD template per product (or a shared one with a product-specific Section 9), **versioned by release**.
- Store templates centrally where your team can find them; additional handbook materials are on www.presales-handbook.com.
- Create the deal's OSD by copying the current template into the deal folder.

---

## Storing the OSD and linking it from the CRM

1. Create the OSD and place the document(s) in the respective **deal folder**.
2. If your CRM has an OSD field (or a notes/link field), paste a **link** there:
   - **Single product** → link to the OSD document.
   - **Multi-product / multiple docs** → link to the folder where the OSD documents are stored.
   - **No OSD required** → record *"No OSD required"*.
3. Naming convention: `OSD-[Opportunity Name]-v1.x`.
4. Best practice: reference the CRM opportunity link and the OSD when sharing with other groups (Sales, PS).
