## PMCB — Group Behaviour Change

> **Credit.** Study note in our own words. TOC thinking processes originate with Eliyahu M. Goldratt. This tool follows the Black Belt in Thinking programme ([blackbeltinthinking.com](https://blackbeltinthinking.com/)).

*Use when a whole group behaves the same way and you want to understand or change it. PMCB covers most group cases. Use ABC to sharpen specific consequences or for behaviour not tied to any policy.*

### What it is

People act on how they are measured. When a group behaves uniformly, a chain is usually behind it:

| Part | What it is |
|------|-----------|
| **Policy** | A rule or expectation, formal or informal |
| **Measure** | What is tracked to check the policy |
| **Consequence** | What happens to people based on the measure |
| **Behaviour** | What the group actually does |

Asking the group to "just do something else" means fighting the chain every day. Changing one link shifts the whole group at once.

### When to use it in PreSales

- A customer's buying committee all push for the cheapest option, whatever the value case says.
- Regional sales teams all discount heavily in the last week of the quarter.
- A customer's operations team resists every process change the pilot needs.
- An injection is blocked because a group's measures reward the old behaviour.

### Steps — work backwards

- [ ] Name the group behaviour you do not want.
- [ ] Find the consequences that drive it.
- [ ] Find the measure those consequences are based on.
- [ ] Find the policy behind that measure.
- [ ] Decide which link to change. It is usually the **measure**, the weakest link, not a new penalty.
- [ ] Check the new measure against the policy's intent. Would gaming it still produce the wanted behaviour?

### B2B example

A customer's procurement team rejects every multi-year proposal.
- Policy: control spend. (Reasonable.)
- Measure: first-year contract value against budget. (The weak link.)
- Consequence: buyers are ranked on first-year savings.
- Behaviour: pick the lowest year-one price and refuse longer terms.

Injection to propose: the champion and CFO agree to judge this purchase on three-year total cost. The same policy and consequences now reward the option that is cheaper over the contract's life.

### PMCB and Druids

A goal violation at the top of a druid is usually a policy not being met. PMCB explains the loopback: violation → renewed focus on the policy → tighter measurement → harsher consequences → more of the same behaviour. It also explains why opposite sides of a druid are often enforced by different departments with conflicting measures, such as finance against customer service. Aligning those chains can remove a cause without a full injection.
