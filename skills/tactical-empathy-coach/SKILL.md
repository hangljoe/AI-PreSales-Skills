# Tactical Empathy Coach

Turns objections into structured conversations. The backbone is the handbook's objection
framework (ch. 15): type the objection, then **Listen → Empathise → Probe → Address →
Confirm resolution**. Tactical empathy (Chris Voss: labels, mirrors, calibrated questions,
accusation audit) is the how-to inside Listen, Empathise and Probe. NVC is the repair tool
when the relationship is strained.

## Step 1 — Intake

Collect (ask if not provided):
1. **The exact objection or pushback** (quote verbatim if possible)
2. **Context**: Who said it? What stage is the deal at? What had just happened?
3. **Your relationship with this person**: New contact / established rapport / senior stakeholder
4. **What you know about their real concern** (what might be behind the words?)
5. **Your product's honest position** on the point raised: fully covered, workaround, roadmap, or a real gap

---

## Step 2 — Type the objection (handbook ch. 15, "Overview of Objections")

| Type | What it is | Tell-tale sign | Coaching consequence |
|------|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| **Latent** | Hidden, not yet voiced; surfaces later in the buying process | Silence from a stakeholder, stalled next steps, vague "we'll see" | Surface it early: accusation audit and labels |
| **Expressed** | Voiced directly: capability, price, implementation | A clear statement or question | Work the full flow below |
| **Valid** | A real gap or genuine fit concern | Your product truly does not do it (yet) | Acknowledge it upfront in Address; never spin it |
| **Smoke screen** | A minor or distracting concern masking a deeper one | Objection shifts when answered; answer lands but mood does not change | Probe for the real one before addressing anything |

Also tag it **technical** (integration, scalability, security) or **non-technical** (price,
incumbent vendor, implementation effort). One objection can carry two tags, e.g.
expressed + valid, or expressed + smoke screen.

---

## Step 3 — Listen (Voss: accusation audit, mirror, silence)

Before responding, grasp the whole objection. Give the customer time to finish; a moment of
silence helps them think (ch. 15, "Listen Actively").

**Accusation audit — prepare it, then say it aloud.** List the 3–5 negative things they
might be thinking about you, your product or this deal. Voss's point is to name them out
loud, early, before the other side can:

```
Prepare:                              Say it:
- "This is too expensive"             "You're probably thinking this will cost more than it's worth,
- "We tried this before"               that you've been burned by a rollout like this before, and that
- "My team will resist it"             your team has no time for another tool."
- "I'm not sure I trust the vendor"   [Pause. Let them respond.]
```

**Mirror** — repeat the last 1–3 words with rising intonation, then stay quiet:
`"[Last 1-3 words of their objection]...?"` [Silence]
Rule: three seconds of silence after a mirror. Resist the urge to fill it.

---

## Step 4 — Empathise (Voss: label)

Recognise that the concern is valid for them. This is not agreeing with it.

- Handbook phrasing is fine: **"I understand where you are coming from."** (ch. 15)
- A label goes one step further: "It seems like…", "It sounds like…", "It feels like…",
  naming the emotion or concern and ending open so they can correct you:
  `"It sounds like [emotion/concern you inferred] — is that right?"`

**Rule:** empathy names *their* concern. Ban only hollow agreement that names nothing:
"I understand how you feel", "I totally get it", "great question". Those sound scripted and
close the conversation instead of opening it.

---

## Step 5 — Probe (Voss: calibrated questions)

Find the root before answering (ch. 15, "Ask Probing Questions"). If the solution "seems too
complex", is it the user interface, the integration, or features they don't need?

Calibrated questions start with **How** or **What**, never **Why** (it triggers defensiveness):
```
"What part of it feels most complex from where you sit?"
"How would this affect your team if we got it wrong?"
"What would need to be true for [the obstacle] not to be a blocker?"
```
For a suspected smoke screen: "Suppose we solved that completely. What else would stop you?"

---

## Step 6 — Address (honest acknowledgement of real gaps)

Give a direct, honest answer (ch. 15, "Address the Objection"):
- **Covered** → show or prove it; point to the specific capability and evidence.
- **Valid gap** → acknowledge it upfront, then discuss a workaround, partner option, or
  roadmap item. Never promise dates you cannot confirm; tag roadmap claims 🟡 or 🔴.
- **Smoke screen** → do not over-answer the surface point; go back to Probe.

**Technical objections (ch. 15, "Common Technical Objections"):**

| Objection | Address with |
|-----------|--------------|
| "Your solution does not integrate with our existing systems." | The integrations you have today; custom options or middleware; integrations on the roadmap |
| "We've heard you have performance / scalability issues." | Case studies from customers at a similar scale; recent performance work; offer a performance test or PoC |
| "The security features seem lacking." | Security measures, certifications and compliance standards; third-party audits; upcoming security enhancements |

Non-technical (price, incumbent vendor, long implementation) → see the quick reference and
handoffs below.

---

## Step 7 — Confirm resolution

Addressing is not the end. Check in (ch. 15, "Confirm Resolution"):
`"Does that answer the concern, or is there something we still need to work through?"`

If the objection persists: propose a pilot or trial, bring in a subject-matter expert, or
schedule a follow-up after their internal discussion (ch. 15, "Handling Persistent Objections").

---

## Step 8 — Three response options and the best move

For the opening move, generate all three and recommend one, with the reason:
- **Option A: Label** — "It seems like…"
- **Option B: Mirror** — last 1–3 words, then silence
- **Option C: Calibrated question** — How / What

Then give the follow-up sequence: what to say if they still push back, what the "no" might
really mean (often "not yet" or "I need more information"), and when to switch to NVC.

---

## Step 9 — NVC frame (use when the relationship needs repair)

Observation-Feeling-Need-Request. Use when tactical empathy alone isn't opening things up.

```
Observation: "When I heard [exact words they said]..."
Feeling: "I noticed I felt [curious / concerned / uncertain]..."
Need: "...because what matters to me is [your actual need: clarity / their success / a fair deal]."
Request: "Would you be willing to tell me more about what's driving that concern?"
```

NVC works in writing too. Use it in a follow-up email after a tense call.

## Common B2B objections — quick reference

| Objection | Likely type / real concern | Best first move |
|-----------|---------------------------|-----------------|
| "It's too expensive" | Expressed; budget not approved, fear of justifying it internally | Label: "It seems like the budget conversation internally is the real challenge here." |
| "We already have [incumbent system]" | Expressed, often smoke screen; fear of disruption | Mirror: "Already have [incumbent system]...?" then "How is that working for the [specific pain] we discussed?" |
| "We tried automation before and it didn't stick" | Latent turned expressed; past failure | Say the accusation audit aloud, then label: "It sounds like you've been burned before." |
| "We need to involve procurement" | Expressed; paper process starting, not a real no | Calibrated: "What does procurement need to see to make this straightforward for them?" |
| "Can you do a POC?" | Not yet convinced, or a delay tactic | Label, then: "What would a successful PoC look like for you?" |
| "The timing isn't right" | Often smoke screen; no compelling event | Calibrated: "What would need to change for the timing to work?" |
| "Your competitor does this too" | Hasn't seen the differentiator, or negotiating | Mirror: "Does this too...?" then "What would make the choice obvious?" |
| "We need to think about it" | Smoke screen; missing information or buy-in | Label: "It sounds like there's something you're not yet sure about." |

Offer the matching handoff in one line after Step 8:

| Objection is really about... | Hand off to |
|------------------------------|-------------|
| Price ("too expensive", "the other vendor is cheaper") | `pricing-positioning` (say *pricing conversation*); discounts, terms and concessions → `negotiation-prep` |

## Step 10 — Role-play mode (optional)

If the user wants to rehearse, enter a live role-play:
- User plays themselves
- Claude plays the objecting stakeholder
- After each exchange, Claude steps out to coach: "Good. Now try labelling the emotion behind what I just said."

Practising with colleagues in role-play is part of the handbook's objection-handling mindset (ch. 15).

## Output template

```
OBJECTION: "[verbatim]"
Type: [latent / expressed / valid / smoke screen] + [technical / non-technical]
Accusation audit (say aloud): "[2-3 sentences]"
Listen: [mirror or silence cue]
Empathise: [label or "I understand where you are coming from" + named concern]
Probe: [1-2 calibrated questions]
Address: [honest answer; gap acknowledged if valid; evidence]
Confirm: "[check-in question]"
Options A / B / C: [...]   Best first move: [A/B/C] because [...]
If they still push back: [...]
Handoff: [skill/command, if any]
```

## Handoff

- Price or terms → `pricing-positioning`, then `negotiation-prep` for discounts and concessions.
- A named competitor → `competitive-battlecard`.
- A PoC request → `/presales:deal:poc-plan` (run its PoC gate first).
- The ROI numbers → `/presales:value:roi-case`, then `business-case-stress-tester`.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Objection typed (latent / expressed / valid / smoke screen; technical / non-technical)
- [ ] Accusation audit prepared AND phrased to be said aloud
- [ ] Flow complete: Listen → Empathise → Probe → Address → Confirm resolution
- [ ] Valid gaps acknowledged upfront, with workaround or roadmap tagged 🟡/🔴
- [ ] All three opening options generated (label, mirror, calibrated question); best move recommended with reasoning
- [ ] No "why" questions; no hollow agreement ("I understand how you feel")
- [ ] Follow-up move provided in case the first option doesn't open things up
- [ ] NVC frame offered if relationship repair is needed
- [ ] Handoff offered when the objection is about price, a competitor, a PoC, or the numbers
