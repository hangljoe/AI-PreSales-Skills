# Deal Pre-Qualification — Warm Question Drafting  *(authoritative for tone)*

This file governs **every word that leaves the tool and goes to an AE or sales leader.** The whole point
of `deal-prequal` is that a leader can forward the drafted message **unedited** and the AE feels *backed*,
not audited. If a draft could be read as "you didn't do your job," it's wrong — rewrite it.

Grounded in the same tactical-empathy / field-comms principles the library uses elsewhere: partnership
language, an accusation audit that pre-empts the sting, calibrated *how/what* questions, and brevity.

---

## 1. The posture: "help me help you"

The leader is offering to invest scarce SC resource. Frame every ask as **protecting and strengthening the
AE's deal**, not as a compliance check:

- The gap belongs to *the deal*, never to the person: *"a couple of things we'd want nailed before we throw
  SC weight behind it"* — not *"you're missing…"*.
- Lead with what's **good** about the deal — genuine, specific, not flattery.
- Make the ask **easy and optional-feeling**: *"even a rough steer helps"*, *"no need to write an essay"*.
- Give an out: *"if you've already got this, just point me to it."*
- Assume competence and good faith. The AE has been busy closing, not stalling.

---

## 2. Accusation audit (defuse before you ask)

Name the possible sting first, gently, so it can't land:

> *"I know this deal's been moving fast and the admin isn't the fun part —"*
> *"You may already have all of this in your head —"*
> *"Totally get that discovery is still early here —"*

Then ask. The audit makes even a long gap list feel like teamwork.

---

## 3. Calibrated questions (how / what, not why)

Ask **how** and **what**, never **why** (which reads as challenge). Open-ended so the AE volunteers detail:

- ✅ *"What's the business pain the sponsor keeps coming back to?"*
- ✅ *"How does a purchase like this typically get signed off there?"*
- ✅ *"Who ultimately owns the budget for this?"*
- 🚫 *"Why haven't you identified the economic buyer?"*
- 🚫 *"Did you actually confirm the pain?"*

---

## 4. Per-gap question bank (warm phrasings by MEDDPICC element)

Pick the **2–3 highest-value** gaps — never dump all eight. Each below is send-ready.

| Gap (element) | Warm, ready-to-send question |
|---------------|------------------------------|
| **Metrics** | *"Do we have a rough number on what this is costing them today — even a ballpark? It makes the business case land much harder."* |
| **Economic Buyer** | *"Who ultimately owns the budget / signs this off? Handy to know who we're building toward."* |
| **Decision Criteria** | *"Any sense of what they'll judge the solution on — the two or three things that matter most to them?"* |
| **Decision Process** | *"What does their path to a decision look like — rough steps or timing? Helps us pace the SC effort right."* |
| **Paper Process** | *"Any early read on the procurement / security path? Good to surface early so nothing stalls us later."* |
| **Implicated Pain** | *"What's the pain the sponsor keeps returning to — and is there an event driving urgency (audit, go-live, renewal)?"* |
| **Champion** | *"Who's our strongest internal advocate, and are they actively selling this when we're not in the room?"* |
| **Competition** | *"Anyone else in the mix, or an incumbent we'd be replacing? Shapes how we position the demo."* |
| **Intake form** | *"Could you add a line or two on the ask and the business reason to the SC request? Lets me line up the right resource fast."* |

---

## 5. Message skeleton

Keep it short — 5–8 sentences for email, 2–3 for chat. Structure:

1. **Warm open + specific positive** — *"Really like the look of the <Account> opportunity — the <X> angle is strong."*
2. **Accusation audit** — pre-empt the sting (§2).
3. **Frame** — *"Before we put SC time behind it, a couple of things would help us hit it hard:"*
4. **The 2–3 asks** — from §4, bulleted, specific to this deal.
5. **Easy out** — *"If you've already got these, just point me at where."*
6. **Warm close** — *"Keen to get behind this one — thanks for the steer."*

### Worked example (email, 🟡 A few gaps)

> **Subject:** Quick one on <Account> — want to get SC behind it
>
> Hi <AE>,
>
> Really like the <Account> opportunity — the reporting-automation angle is exactly where we're strong, and the
> timing looks good.
>
> I know discovery's still live and the admin's never the fun part — you may well have this in your head
> already. Before we commit SC time to the demo, two things would help us make it land:
>
> - Do we have a rough number on what the classification rework is costing them today? Even a ballpark
>   sharpens the business case.
> - Who ultimately owns the budget / signs this off?
>
> If you've already got these, just point me at where and I'll pick them up. Keen to get properly behind
> this one — thanks!
>
> <Leader>

### Worked example (Teams/Slack, 🔵 Too early)

> Hey <AE> — <Account> looks promising 👍 Before I line up SC time, could you give me a quick steer on the
> core business pain the sponsor keeps raising, and who owns the budget? No need for detail — even rough is
> great. Happy to jump on a 10-min call if that's easier. Want to make sure we back this one properly.

---

## 6. Channel variants

- **Email** — full skeleton (§5); subject line frames it as *support*, not *chase* ("want to get SC behind
  it", never "missing information").
- **Teams / Slack** — compress to open + 1–2 asks + easy out; one emoji max; offer a 10-min call as the out.

---

## 7. Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| "a couple of things would help us hit this hard" | "the following fields are incomplete" |
| Lead with a genuine positive | Open with the gap |
| Ask the top 2–3 only | List all eight MEDDPICC gaps |
| "if you've got this already, point me at it" | Imply they've dropped the ball |
| Frame gaps as the deal's, tone as the team's | Personalise the gap to the AE |
| Offer a quick call as the low-effort path | Demand written answers |

**Golden test before sending any draft:** *would the AE feel supported, or judged?* If there's any doubt,
soften and shorten. A leader's follow-up should make the AE want to work with SC again.
