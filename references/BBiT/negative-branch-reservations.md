# Negative Branch Reservations (NBR)

> **Credit.** Study note in our own words. The Theory of Constraints thinking processes originate with Eliyahu M. Goldratt. This tool, as used here, follows the Black Belt in Thinking (BBiT) programme ([blackbeltinthinking.com](https://blackbeltinthinking.com/)). The method remains the work of its authors.

## What it is

An NBR maps a worrying side effect of an idea you *want* to pursue. You link the idea to the negative with cause-and-effect steps. Then you decide whether the negative is **legitimate** (real, so mitigate or accept it) or a **fear** (the logic does not hold). It starts from a wanted action, never from a pure negative.

NBR or druid? A druid is a recurring loop where both sides have happened. An NBR is usually a one-off prediction.

## When to use it in PreSales

- Someone objects "if we offer a pilot, they'll never buy the full licence".
- You want to hand demo delivery to a partner and fear quality loss.
- A customer fears your solution will disrupt their month-end close.

## Steps (N-B-R)

- [ ] **Negative:** put the wanted action at the bottom and the significant negative at the top. Drop minor worries.
- [ ] **Branch:** connect them with four or more "If…then" steps. Note assumptions as you go. If the chain is hard to build, the worry is probably unlikely.
- [ ] **Resolve, part 1:** check logic, assumptions and magnitude. A small slip rarely causes a large failure. If a link fails, it was a fear.
- [ ] **Resolve, part 2:** if legitimate, can you simply accept it given the upside?
- [ ] **Inject:** if not, grade each step positive, neutral or negative. Place the injection after the last positive and before the first negative. The injection only needs to remove the negative.
- [ ] If the negative can arise by several paths, resolve each one. For negatives that only appear if conditions change, keep the branch as a contingency plan.

## B2B example

Action: offer a paid 60-day pilot.
Branch: pilot is scoped narrowly → customer gets value from the narrow scope → sees no reason to expand → no full licence (negative).
Grading: the first two steps are positive, the third is negative.
Injection placed between them: "Pilot success criteria include a signed expansion decision at day 60, tied to agreed metrics."

## Common errors

- Adding the idea's benefits into the branch. Weigh benefits before the NBR.
- Biased causality: loving the idea shrinks the risk, disliking it inflates it. Bearing the downside while others get the upside also inflates it.
