# PMCB: Group Behaviour Change

> **Credit.** Study note in our own words. The Theory of Constraints thinking processes originate with Eliyahu M. Goldratt. This tool, as used here, follows the Black Belt in Thinking (BBiT) programme ([blackbeltinthinking.com](https://blackbeltinthinking.com/)). The method remains the work of its authors.

## What it is

When a whole group behaves the same way, a chain is usually behind it: a **Policy** sets the expectation, a **Measure** tracks it, **Consequences** follow from the measure, and **Behaviour** follows the consequences. People act on how they are measured.

PMCB is the group-level lens. ABC (`abc-behaviour-change.md`) is the individual-level lens and can sharpen the consequences in a PMCB.

- Policies can be formal (written rules), informal ("how we do things here") or internal (personal rules).
- A good measure fits the policy, has no loopholes, is visible, and does not conflict with other measures.
- Effective consequences matter to people, scale with the stakes, align with the measure, and are applied consistently and visibly.

## When to use it in PreSales

- A whole SC team skips discovery notes in the CRM.
- A customer's planners all bypass a new system you are selling into.
- A druid loops because two departments enforce opposing measures.

## Steps (work backwards)

- [ ] Name the unwanted group behaviour.
- [ ] Find the consequences that drive it.
- [ ] Find the measure those consequences are based on.
- [ ] Find the policy behind that measure.
- [ ] Locate the broken link. It is most often the measure, not the policy.
- [ ] Fix that link instead of adding punishment. Check for natural or internal consequences that may work against the change.

## B2B example

Behaviour: SCs rarely write up discovery in the CRM.
Consequences: no one notices missing notes; time spent on notes takes time from demos, which are praised.
Measure: number of demos delivered per quarter.
Policy: "SCs should maximise customer-facing time."
Fix: change the measure to "opportunities with a completed discovery summary before the first demo". The policy stays; the behaviour shifts.

## PMCB in druids

A goal violation is usually a policy not being met. It increases attention on a measure, which tightens consequences and drives the opposite behaviour. When each side of a druid is policed by a different department, aligning their measures can break the loop.
