# Choosing a Subject and Applying Scrutiny (CLRs)

> **Credit.** Study note in our own words. The Theory of Constraints thinking processes and the Categories of Legitimate Reservation originate with Eliyahu M. Goldratt. This tool, as used here, follows the Black Belt in Thinking (BBiT) programme ([blackbeltinthinking.com](https://blackbeltinthinking.com/)). The method remains the work of its authors.

## Part 1: Choosing a subject

A **subject** is an ongoing area with several recurring problems ("our POC process"), analysed to find the core problem behind them. It is not a goal ("win Deal X by Q3"). Goals suit the PRT and the cloud; subjects suit the CRT.

Test: has this happened before, and will it happen again? If you start by listing obstacles, you probably have a goal.

Checks for a good subject:
- [ ] **Intuition:** you know the area well (roughly six months or more).
- [ ] **Care:** you are invested enough to finish.
- [ ] **Size:** three to five distinct issues. One druid is too small; "the whole business" is too big.
- [ ] **Influence:** you can actually cause change.
- [ ] One consistent perspective throughout (business view or personal view, not both). Avoid highly emotional topics while learning.

PreSales example: "How our team runs technical evaluations" is a subject. "Win the Acme POC" is a goal.

## Part 2: Scrutiny with CLRs

Scrutiny is proofreading for logic. The **Categories of Legitimate Reservation (CLRs)** are a structured set of checks on cause-and-effect links. The two used most:

- **Clarity on the entity:** is each statement clear and unambiguous on its own?
- **Clarity on the connection:** can you see how the cause produces the effect?

Further CLRs test existence (is it true?), sufficiency (is the cause enough?), additional cause, cause-effect reversal and predicted effect. Use them when stakes are high.

## Steps

- [ ] Review first, fix later. Flag issues with a short token ("clarity on 'users'") instead of editing on the spot.
- [ ] Be specific: flag the exact word or link.
- [ ] Limit flags to about three to five per druid side or tree section. Big issues first.
- [ ] Match rigour to stakes. Iterate in several short rounds.

## B2B example

Link: "If the demo is generic, then the deal slips."
Clarity flags: "generic" (which parts?) and "slips" (by how long, and why?).
Rewrite: "If the demo shows no workflow from the customer's top two pains, then the economic buyer does not approve the next step."
