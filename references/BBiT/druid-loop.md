# The Druid Loop: Recurring Conflicts

> **Credit.** Study note in our own words. The Theory of Constraints thinking processes originate with Eliyahu M. Goldratt. The Druid tool was co-developed by James Powell and Peter Cronin and is taught in the Black Belt in Thinking (BBiT) programme ([blackbeltinthinking.com](https://blackbeltinthinking.com/)). The method remains the work of its authors.

## What it is

A druid maps an oscillation between two behaviours. Doing A causes a side effect that grows until it feels unacceptable (a goal violation). That pushes you to B. B causes its own goal violation, which pushes you back to A. Mapping both sides turns "you versus me" into "us versus the loop".

It only fits problems that keep coming back. A one-off worry belongs in an NBR or a cloud.

## When to use it in PreSales

- The team swings between "custom demo for every prospect" and "standard demo only".
- Management swings between tight deal-review control and hands-off trust.
- Pricing swings between generous discounting and a strict no-discount rule.

## Steps

- [ ] Name the behaviour pair: two things you actually do, which you cannot do at once.
- [ ] Start with the side whose downside you know best. Build "If…then" steps up to its goal violation.
- [ ] Check that goal violation is strong enough to force the switch.
- [ ] Build the other side the same way, up to its own goal violation.
- [ ] Run the validation checklist below. Then target one link with an injection (see `injections.md`).

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Both base entities are behaviours, not concepts or outcomes.
- [ ] They are mutually exclusive in practice (time, people, budget).
- [ ] Each link holds when read on its own.
- [ ] Both sides have really happened, not just been feared.
- [ ] Each goal violation is serious, and it raises pressure for the opposite behaviour.

## B2B example

- Side A: custom demo per prospect → SCs spend days on prep → fewer deals covered → pipeline coverage drops (goal violation) → push to standard demos.
- Side B: standard demo only → demos miss prospect-specific pains → win rate on competitive deals falls (goal violation) → push back to custom demos.
- Possible injection: "A modular demo covers 80% of prospect-specific pains with under two hours of prep."

## Common errors

- **Repetition:** two steps that say the same thing.
- **Premature looping:** the goal violation already names the opposite behaviour. Stop at the negative itself.
- **Failed solution:** one side's only downside is "it doesn't work". That is not a real second approach.
- **Tangent:** the chain drifts to a different topic from the base behaviour.
- **Stopping short:** ending before the negative that actually triggers the switch.
