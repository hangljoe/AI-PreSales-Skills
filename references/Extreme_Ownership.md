# Extreme Ownership — Study Note for PreSales

> **Source and credit.** Study note in our own words on *Extreme Ownership: How U.S. Navy SEALs Lead and Win* by Jocko Willink and Leif Babin (St. Martin's Press, 2015; new edition 2017). The ideas are the authors' work; read the book for the full principles and the stories behind them: [us.macmillan.com/books/9781250183866/extremeownership](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9781250183866/extremeownership/). This note only maps the principles to Solution Consultant work.

The core stance: you own every outcome that touches your mission, including the parts that were not directly your fault. No excuses, no blame. The energy goes into what you can still change.

---

## The principles, applied to PreSales

**1. Own the outcome completely.** Responsibility for the result sits with you, not with the market, the product or the AE.
*SC application:* In a lost-deal debrief, open with what you would do differently (discovery depth, demo focus, missed stakeholder) before discussing anyone else's part.

**2. There are no bad teams, only leaders who accept bad standards.** A team's performance reflects what its leader tolerates.
*SC application:* If demos on your team routinely run over time or skip the "tell" step, that is a standard you allowed. Fix the standard, not the individuals.

**3. Believe in the mission.** You cannot convince others of something you have not understood and accepted yourself.
*SC application:* When a deal strategy or pricing decision feels wrong, ask the "why" until you can defend it to the customer. Otherwise it shows.

**4. Check the ego.** Pride blocks honest assessment and cooperation.
*SC application:* When the customer's architect challenges your design, treat it as information about the deal, not an attack on your expertise.

**5. Cover and move.** Teams win together; internal "us versus them" loses deals.
*SC application:* Sales, PreSales, PS and product are one team on a deal. Unblock the other function instead of documenting why they blocked you.

**6. Keep it simple.** Plans and messages must be understood by everyone who executes them.
*SC application:* If the mutual action plan or the solution story needs a long explanation, simplify it until the champion can repeat it alone.

**7. Prioritise and execute.** Under pressure, pick the single most important problem, solve it, then move to the next.
*SC application:* In a stuck deal, name the one constraint (often access to the economic buyer or an unproven use case) and put your effort there first.

**8. Decentralised command.** People at every level lead within a clearly understood intent.
*SC application:* Give junior SCs the "why" and the boundaries of a POC, then let them run it. Check results, not every keystroke.

**9. Plan with intent and contingencies.** A good plan states the purpose, the desired end state, roles, risks and fallback options.
*SC application:* Every POC or demo plan names the success criteria, the owner of each step and what you do if the key integration fails.

**10. Lead up and down the chain.** Keep your own leaders informed and ask for what you need; explain the "why" to those you lead.
*SC application:* Do not wait for your manager to ask about deal risk. Brief them early, with a clear request (exec sponsor, specialist, budget).

**11. Decide under uncertainty.** You will never have complete information. Decide, act, and adjust.
*SC application:* Go/no-go on an RFP or POC with the evidence you have today, and state the conditions under which you would reverse the call.

**12. Discipline creates freedom.** Consistent routines free you to adapt when things go wrong. Every principle also has a balancing opposite (confident, not arrogant; decisive, not reckless).
*SC application:* A fixed demo-prep and debrief routine is what lets you improvise well when the customer changes the agenda live.

---

## Working habits for SCs

- **Brief, execute, debrief, adjust.** After every major customer meeting, spend ten minutes on what worked, what did not and what changes next time.
- **Stay calm.** Your tone in a tense deal review sets the tone for the room.
- **Train the team.** Capability gaps on your team are a leadership task, not an enablement-department problem.

## Useful self-check questions

- What do I own here, even if it is not my fault?
- What is the one priority right now?
- What must I simplify?
- What am I tolerating that I should not?
